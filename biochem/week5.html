1. Enzyme basics

Enzymes are biocatalysts that accelerate biochemical reactions. They are not consumed during catalysis and can participate in repeated reaction cycles. Enzymatic reactions occur in specialized pockets called active sites.

Specificity: group specificity means an enzyme can act on related substrates (alkaline phosphatase); absolute specificity means an enzyme acts almost exclusively on one substrate (glucose oxidase for β-D-glucose).

Regulation: reversible covalent modification, allosteric modulators, proteolytic activation, noncovalent regulatory proteins, and regulatory cascades.

4. Enzyme catalysis

Thermodynamically favored reactions may still be slow because of an activation-energy barrier. Catalysts lower that barrier.

  • Change reaction rate, not the equilibrium constant.
  • Speed forward and reverse reactions.
  • Bind substrates transiently.
  • Some RNA molecules are catalytic: ribozymes.
Exam pearl: enzymes help equilibrium be reached faster; they do not change where equilibrium lies.

5. Michaelis-Menten kinetics

The simple model is E + S ⇌ ES → product. At steady state, [ES] is relatively constant. Initial velocity V₀ rises as substrate concentration rises and approaches Vmax as enzyme becomes saturated.

ParameterMeaning
VmaxMaximum reaction velocity at saturation; proportional to total enzyme concentration.
KmSubstrate concentration required for ½ Vmax.
Low KmHigher substrate affinity.
High KmLower substrate affinity.
KcatTurnover number: substrate molecules converted per enzyme molecule per second at saturation.
Kcat/KmSpecificity constant / catalytic efficiency.

Standard Michaelis-Menten behavior gives a hyperbolic curve; cooperative enzymes give a sigmoidal curve.

Original professor visuals: Slides 23 and 26 — Michaelis-Menten/saturation graphs. These will be displayed exactly from the PowerPoint.

6. Lineweaver-Burk

1/V₀ = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax
  • Y-intercept = 1/Vmax
  • X-intercept = −1/Km
  • Slope = Km/Vmax
Original professor visual: Slide 29 — Lineweaver-Burk plot.

7. Competitive inhibition

Competes with substrate for the active site and can often be overcome with enough substrate.

Km ↑   |   Vmax unchanged
Methotrexate: competitive inhibitor of dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), competing with folate.

Lineweaver-Burk: same y-intercept, x-intercept shifts toward zero, slope increases.

Original professor visuals: Slides 32 and 34 — competitive inhibition curves.

8. Noncompetitive inhibition

Binds away from the active site; increasing substrate cannot overcome pure noncompetitive inhibition.

Km unchanged   |   Vmax ↓

Lineweaver-Burk: same x-intercept, higher y-intercept.

Original professor visuals: Slides 36 and 38 — noncompetitive inhibition curves.

9. Uncompetitive inhibition

Binds only the enzyme-substrate complex, forming inactive ESI.

Km ↓   |   Vmax ↓

Lineweaver-Burk lines are parallel because Km and Vmax decrease proportionally.

Original professor visuals: Slides 40 and 42 — uncompetitive inhibition curves.

10. Suicide inhibition

Irreversible. A substrate-like inhibitor is processed by the enzyme into a reactive intermediate that permanently inactivates the enzyme.

Examples: penicillin inhibits bacterial transpeptidases; the lecture also shows allopurinol as a suicide inhibitor of xanthine oxidase.
Original professor visuals: Slides 43–44 — suicide inhibition, penicillin, and allopurinol. Slide 44 is labeled information/understanding only.

11. Enzyme activators

The lecture notes that an enzyme activator can increase Vmax upon binding; V-type enzymes are named for changes in Vmax during allosteric activation.

Original professor visual: Slide 45 — enzyme activator note.

Practice Questions

Answer before opening.

1. Which inhibition raises Km but leaves Vmax unchanged?

Competitive.

2. Which inhibition lowers Vmax while leaving Km unchanged?

Pure noncompetitive.

3. Which inhibitor binds only ES?

Uncompetitive.

4. What does Km represent?

Substrate concentration at ½Vmax.

5. What does a low Km indicate?

Higher substrate affinity.

6. Does adding more enzyme change Vmax?

Yes. Vmax is proportional to total enzyme concentration.

7. What is Kcat/Km?

Specificity constant / overall catalytic efficiency.

8. What are the Lineweaver-Burk intercepts?

Y = 1/Vmax; X = −1/Km.

9. Methotrexate competitively inhibits what enzyme?

Dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR).

10. Why is suicide inhibition irreversible?

The enzyme processes a substrate-like inhibitor into a reactive intermediate that permanently inactivates it.
