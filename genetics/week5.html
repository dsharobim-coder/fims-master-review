1. Directionality and base pairing

DNA strands are antiparallel. Adjacent nucleotides are joined through phosphodiester bonds between the 3′-OH and 5′ phosphate. Complementary strands pair A with T and G with C by hydrogen bonds.

DNA polymerase READS template 3′ → 5′ and SYNTHESIZES new DNA 5′ → 3′

2. Three proposed replication models

ModelPrediction
ConservativeOriginal parental duplex remains together; a completely new duplex forms.
SemiconservativeEach daughter duplex contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand.
DispersiveOld and new DNA are mixed in segments within daughter strands.

3. Meselson–Stahl

Bacteria were grown in heavy ¹⁵N, labeling both strands, then transferred to light ¹⁴N.

  • Generation 1: every DNA molecule had one heavy old strand + one light new strand → all DNA had intermediate density.
  • Generation 2: half the DNA was light/light and half was heavy/light.
  • In later generations, the hybrid fraction became progressively smaller.
Conclusion: DNA replication is semiconservative.

4. oriC and bidirectional replication

Most mechanistic bacterial replication work comes from E. coli. Its circular chromosome has one main origin of replication, oriC. Replication proceeds in both directions from oriC, producing two replication forks that eventually meet on the opposite side.

  • DnaA boxes = sites where initiator DnaA binds.
  • AT-rich region = easier to separate because A–T has 2 hydrogen bonds.
  • GATC methylation sites = regulatory sites near oriC.

5. Initiation machinery

  1. DnaA, with DiaA and IHF, binds the DnaA-box region and bends the origin DNA.
  2. This promotes opening of the AT-rich duplex-unwinding element.
  3. DnaC interacts with the initiation complex and helps recruit/load DnaB helicase.
  4. DnaB/helicase unwinds the parental duplex and promotes replication-fork movement.
  5. Topoisomerase II relieves supercoiling ahead of the fork by transient breakage/rejoining.
  6. Single-strand binding proteins prevent the separated templates from reannealing.
Why topoisomerase is needed: helicase opening the helix creates overwinding/supercoiling ahead of the fork. Topoisomerase acts as a swivel by transiently breaking and resealing DNA.

6. Primase

DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to an existing 3′-OH. Primase solves this by synthesizing a short RNA primer, about 10–12 nucleotides in the bacterial lecture. The primer is complementary to the DNA template and creates a short RNA–DNA hybrid.

7. DNA Pol III and DNA Pol I

EnzymeRole
DNA polymerase IIIMain bacterial replication holoenzyme; synthesizes leading and lagging daughter DNA.
DNA polymerase IRemoves RNA primers and replaces them with DNA.

Incoming nucleotides are dNTPs. The 3′-OH of the growing strand reacts with the 5′ phosphate of the incoming nucleotide. Pyrophosphate release helps drive phosphodiester-bond formation.

8. Leading vs lagging strands

LeadingLagging
Continuous synthesis in the same overall direction as fork movement.Discontinuous synthesis in short DNA pieces opposite the overall fork direction.
One main initial primer.Many primers, one for each fragment.
Do not mix up movement and synthesis: the template is read 3′→5′ while the new daughter strand is synthesized 5′→3′.

9. Trombone model

Helicase, primase and DNA Pol III function as a coordinated replisome. Because both polymerases move with the same fork, the lagging-strand template loops back so its polymerase can synthesize 5′→3′ while remaining physically associated with the complex.

The loop grows as an Okazaki fragment is synthesized, collapses when that fragment is completed, and reforms at the next primer.

10. Okazaki fragments

  1. Primase makes an RNA primer.
  2. DNA Pol III extends the fragment 5′→3′.
  3. DNA Pol I removes the RNA primer and replaces it with DNA.
  4. DNA ligase seals the remaining nick by forming a phosphodiester bond.
Primer → Pol III fragment → Pol I replaces RNA → ligase seals

11. Proofreading

If a wrong nucleotide is incorporated, polymerase proofreading removes the incorrect nucleotide and allows the correct one to be inserted. This maintains base-pairing fidelity and reduces replication errors that could become mutations.

12. Regulation at oriC

DnaA-ATP

DnaA-ATP binds DnaA boxes with high affinity and promotes initiation. After initiation, ATP is hydrolyzed to ADP, producing lower-affinity DnaA-ADP. A new round waits until sufficient active DnaA-ATP is available.

GATC methylation / Dam

GATC sequences occur near oriC. DNA adenine methyltransferase (Dam) methylates adenines in GATC. After replication, newly synthesized daughter strands initially contain unmethylated adenines, so the new origin is hemimethylated rather than fully methylated. This altered state is part of the mechanism preventing immediate re-initiation.

Goal: replicate oriC once per cell cycle, not repeatedly after the first copy is made.

13. Termination

On the chromosome opposite oriC are ter sequences. The Tus protein binds ter sites and stops replication forks in an orientation-specific way. The two forks converge in the termination region and the completed products become two circular daughter chromosomes.

Week 5 Rapid Review

  • Replication is semiconservative.
  • Meselson–Stahl: Gen1 all hybrid; Gen2 half hybrid/half light.
  • oriC = one bacterial origin, bidirectional forks.
  • DnaA initiates; DnaC helps load DnaB helicase.
  • Helicase unwinds; topoisomerase relieves supercoils; SSB prevents reannealing.
  • Primase makes RNA primer.
  • Pol III = main synthesis; Pol I = primer removal/replacement; ligase = seals nick.
  • Template read 3′→5′; daughter synthesized 5′→3′.
  • Leading continuous; lagging = Okazaki fragments.
  • Trombone model = lagging template loops.
  • DnaA-ATP + GATC/Dam regulate initiation.
  • Tus/ter terminate bacterial replication.

Week 5 Active Recall

What did Meselson–Stahl prove?

DNA replication is semiconservative.

Gen 1 and Gen 2 pattern?

Gen1 all hybrid; Gen2 half light/light and half hybrid.

DnaA, DnaC, DnaB?

DnaA initiator; DnaC helicase-loading helper; DnaB helicase.

Why topoisomerase?

Relieves supercoiling ahead of the replication fork.

Why primase?

DNA polymerase needs a pre-existing 3′-OH, so primase supplies an RNA primer.

Pol III vs Pol I?

Pol III performs main DNA synthesis; Pol I removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA.

Directionality?

Template read 3′→5′; new strand made 5′→3′.

What are Okazaki fragments?

Short DNA fragments synthesized discontinuously on the lagging strand.

What is the trombone model?

Lagging-strand template loops so both polymerases can move with the same replication fork.

What does ligase do?

Seals phosphodiester nicks between adjacent DNA fragments.

How does GATC methylation help regulate replication?

The newly replicated origin is initially hemimethylated rather than fully methylated, contributing to prevention of immediate re-firing.

Tus/ter?

Tus bound to ter stops forks in the termination region.
