Both systems require helicase, primase, polymerase and ligase functions.

2. Licensing origins in G1

The origin recognition complex (ORC) associates with DNA and marks replication origins. During G1, Cdc6 and Cdt1 associate with the origin and recruit/load MCM2–7, the replicative helicase complex.

ORC + Cdc6 + Cdt1 + MCM2–7 → prereplication complex (pre-RC) → origin is licensed

Licensing means the origin has been prepared to fire later. DNA synthesis has not yet started.

3. Origin firing and replisome assembly

At the G1/S transition, the lecture identifies CDK2–cyclin E and Cdc7–Dbf4 (DDK) as kinases that trigger origin firing through phosphorylation events.

Once firing occurs, Cdc6 and Cdt1 are no longer required and are removed/degraded, helping prevent repeat licensing. MCM + Cdc45 + GINS form the active helicase machinery that unwinds DNA and allows complete replisome assembly.

5. Primer synthesis

Eukaryotic DNA polymerases also require a primer. The Pol α–primase complex first makes an RNA primer of about 10 nucleotides, then Pol α adds a short DNA segment of about 20–30 nucleotides. Pol δ and Pol ε then continue synthesis.

RNA primer (~10 nt) → Pol α short DNA (~20–30 nt) → Pol δ / Pol ε extension

6. Multiple origins and replication bubbles

Because eukaryotic chromosomes are large, many origins fire. Each origin creates a replication bubble with two forks. Bubbles expand until forks encounter DNA already copied by neighboring origins.

7. Primer removal and termination

When a polymerase reaches a region already replicated by a neighboring fork, synthesis stops. RNA primers still need to be removed and the remaining DNA joined.

  • FEN1 (flap endonuclease 1) and RNase H remove RNA primers/flaps from starts of leading strands and Okazaki fragments.
  • DNA fills the remaining gaps.
  • DNA ligase forms phosphodiester bonds between adjacent DNA segments.

8. The end-replication problem

Linear chromosome ends create a special problem because DNA polymerases:

  • only synthesize 5′→3′,
  • require a pre-existing primer/3′-OH,
  • cannot start DNA synthesis from nothing beyond the chromosome end.

When the final RNA primer at the end of a lagging strand is removed, there is no upstream 3′-OH from which conventional DNA polymerase can fill the terminal gap. Without another solution, chromosomes would shorten after every round of replication.

9. Telomeres

Telomeres are repetitive DNA at the ends of eukaryotic chromosomes. Human telomeres contain repeated TTAGGG sequences and a 3′ overhang.

  • They prevent loss of important genetic information as ends shorten.
  • They protect chromosome ends from being treated like broken DNA.
  • They help prevent end-to-end fusion of chromosomes.

10. Telomerase

Telomerase, discovered by Carol Greider and Elizabeth Blackburn, is a ribonucleoprotein complex containing both protein and RNA. Its catalytic protein component is a reverse transcriptase.

  1. Telomerase RNA base-pairs with the chromosome's 3′ telomeric overhang.
  2. The RNA template directs synthesis of a 6-nucleotide telomeric DNA repeat.
  3. Telomerase translocates and repeats the process many times.
  4. Primase/DNA polymerase can then synthesize the complementary strand.
Big picture: telomerase extends the template first, giving normal DNA-synthesis machinery extra sequence to copy so chromosome ends are maintained.

Week 6 Rapid Review

  • Eukaryotic chromosomes = linear + many origins + chromatin.
  • ORC marks origins.
  • Cdc6 + Cdt1 load MCM2–7 in G1 → pre-RC/licensing.
  • CDK2–cyclin E + Cdc7–Dbf4 fire origins.
  • MCM + Cdc45 + GINS = active helicase machinery.
  • Pol α + primase starts; Pol δ/ε extend; Pol γ = mitochondrial DNA.
  • Many origins create replication bubbles that merge.
  • RNase H/FEN1 remove primers/flaps; ligase seals.
  • End-replication problem = terminal primer removal leaves no upstream 3′-OH.
  • Telomere repeat = TTAGGG with 3′ overhang.
  • Telomerase = RNA template + reverse transcriptase protein.

Week 6 Active Recall

What marks eukaryotic origins?

The origin recognition complex (ORC).

What do Cdc6 and Cdt1 do?

Recruit/load MCM2–7 at origins during G1 to license them.

What fires origins?

CDK2–cyclin E and Cdc7–Dbf4 kinase activity.

Active helicase machinery?

MCM with Cdc45 and GINS.

Pol α role?

Works with primase to make RNA primer plus short DNA starter segment.

Pol δ/ε roles?

Major nuclear replication polymerases for continuing daughter-strand synthesis.

Pol γ?

Mitochondrial DNA replication.

Which enzymes remove eukaryotic primers in this lecture?

RNase H and FEN1.

What is the end-replication problem?

After terminal RNA primer removal, there is no upstream 3′-OH to fill the last gap of a linear chromosome.

Human telomere repeat?

TTAGGG.

What is telomerase made of?

An RNA component/template plus protein reverse-transcriptase components.
