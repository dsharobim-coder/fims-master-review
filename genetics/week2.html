Scope

This week continues the professor's Basics of the Cell material with the eukaryotic half: endosymbiotic theory and the location/function of the major animal-cell organelles and structural systems.

1. Endosymbiotic theory

The endosymbiotic theory proposes that an ancestral host cell engulfed bacteria that were not digested. Aerobic bacteria became mitochondria; photosynthetic bacteria became chloroplasts.

  • Mitochondria/chloroplasts contain circular DNA.
  • They have bacterial-like 70S ribosomes.
  • They divide by a process resembling binary fission.
  • They have double membranes.
  • They are similar in size to bacteria.

3. Plasma membrane — endocytosis and exocytosis

Endocytosis

Membrane folds inward and forms a vesicle to bring material into the cell. Used for nutrient uptake, receptor internalization, phagocytosis and fluid uptake. Internalized material may be delivered to lysosomes.

Exocytosis

Vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane and release material outside the cell. Used for secretion of hormones, neurotransmitters, enzymes and extracellular proteins.

Memory: Endo = enter. Exo = exit.

4. Chromatin, nuclear envelope and nuclear pores

Chromatin is DNA associated with proteins, especially histones. DNA wraps around histones to form nucleosomes, helping it fit inside the nucleus and regulating access to genes.

The nuclear envelope is a double membrane. Nuclear pore complexes (NPCs) are the regulated routes into and out of the nucleus. Proteins enter; mRNA and ribosomal subunits leave. Small molecules may diffuse, while large cargo requires controlled transport.

Nuclear structureFunction
NucleolusProduces rRNA and begins assembly of ribosomal subunits.
Nuclear laminaProtein network lining the inner nuclear membrane; supports nuclear shape, anchors pores, organizes chromatin and participates in replication/cell-cycle functions.
Internal nuclear matrixGeneral internal structural scaffolding/organization.
Analogy: nuclear envelope = building wall; pores = guarded doors; nuclear lamina = supporting framework.

5. Centrosomes, centrioles and mitotic microtubules

The centrosome is the major microtubule-organizing center in many animal cells. It usually contains two centrioles plus surrounding pericentriolar material.

During mitosis, spindle microtubules attach to chromosomes, move chromosomes, push spindle poles apart and position division machinery. Their overall job is to ensure each daughter cell receives one complete chromosome set. Failure can produce aneuploidy.

7. Golgi apparatus

Proteins and lipids arrive from the ER at the cis Golgi, move through medial cisternae for modification and leave the trans Golgi, which acts as a distribution center.

A major modification is glycosylation. Carbohydrates added to proteins help folding, stability, protection and targeting. Proteins/lipids/polysaccharides leaving the trans Golgi are sorted into vesicles for lysosomes, the plasma membrane or extracellular release.

ER → cis Golgi → medial Golgi → trans Golgi → destination vesicle

8. Motor proteins and vesicle transport

Kinesin and dynein are ATP-powered motor proteins that move cargo along microtubules. Kinesin usually moves cargo toward the cell periphery; dynein usually moves cargo toward the centrosome/nucleus.

Analogy: microtubules = railroad tracks; motor proteins = engines; vesicles = cargo cars.

11. Eukaryotic cilia and flagella

Eukaryotic cilia and flagella are made of microtubules with the classic 9 + 2 arrangement: nine outer doublets around two central microtubules.

Flagella are usually longer and fewer and move the entire cell, such as sperm. Cilia are shorter and more numerous; they can move cells or move fluid, such as respiratory mucus.

Do not confuse: bacterial flagella rotate; eukaryotic flagella/cilia bend in a wave-like motion.

13. Mitochondria and ATP

Mitochondria produce most ATP used by eukaryotic cells through aerobic cellular respiration. They have an outer membrane and a highly folded inner membrane. The inner folds are cristae, which increase surface area for ATP-producing machinery.

Cells with high energy demands—such as muscle cells, liver cells and neurons—contain many mitochondria.

ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi + usable energy

ATP powers active transport, muscle contraction, biosynthesis, motor proteins and cell division. It is an immediate rechargeable energy carrier, not the body's main long-term energy store.

Week 2 Rapid Review

  • Endosymbiotic evidence: circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, double membranes, fission-like division.
  • Endocytosis enters; exocytosis exits.
  • Chromatin = DNA + protein; nucleolus = rRNA/ribosome assembly; NPC = regulated nuclear traffic.
  • Centrosome = microtubule-organizing center/spindle organization.
  • RER = proteins; SER = lipids/steroids/detox/Ca²⁺.
  • Golgi: cis receive → medial modify → trans ship.
  • Kinesin usually outward; dynein usually inward.
  • Actin = movement/contraction; IF = strength; microtubules = transport/mitosis/cilia.
  • Tight = seal; desmosome = anchor; gap = communicate.
  • Eukaryotic cilia/flagella = 9+2 microtubules.
  • Lysosome digests; peroxisome oxidizes and handles H₂O₂.
  • Mitochondria make ATP; cristae increase inner-membrane surface area.

Week 2 Active Recall

What evidence supports endosymbiotic theory?

Circular DNA, bacterial-like 70S ribosomes, fission-like division, double membranes and bacterial-like size.

Endocytosis vs exocytosis?

Endocytosis brings material in via vesicles; exocytosis releases vesicle contents outside.

What does the nucleolus do?

Produces rRNA and begins ribosomal-subunit assembly.

What is the nuclear lamina?

A protein network under the inner nuclear membrane that supports shape, pores and chromatin organization.

Centrosome function?

Major microtubule-organizing center and spindle organizer.

Rough ER vs smooth ER?

RER has ribosomes and makes/folds proteins; SER makes lipids/steroids, detoxifies and stores Ca²⁺.

Golgi direction?

cis receive → medial modify → trans sort/ship.

Kinesin vs dynein?

Kinesin generally carries cargo toward the periphery; dynein toward the centrosome/nucleus.

Three cytoskeletal systems?

Actin filaments, intermediate filaments and microtubules.

Tight/desmosome/gap junction?

Seal / anchor / communicate.

What does 9+2 refer to?

The microtubule arrangement of eukaryotic cilia and flagella.

Lysosome vs peroxisome?

Lysosome digests with acid hydrolases; peroxisome performs oxidation and H₂O₂ metabolism.
